## 1. Summary

**Model 1** (`bikini_wave.glb`):
- The mesh is identical to the earlier inspected GLB, and the rig's weights are valid.
- The **arms are fused to the body**:
  - the inner upper arm to the ribcage (68–83 % H);
  - the forearms and wrists to the hips (53–58 % H);
  - the fingertips to the thighs (42–45 % H);
  - hair to the shoulders (80–83 % H).
- Under the frozen arm poses and the wave animation, those joins stretch: up to **1,390 edges > 2× and 680 > 4×** their rest length (max **18×**). **Not usable for arm motion** until the mesh is separated.

**Model 2** (`starmodelmeshrig.glb`, regenerated by the user in A-pose, no loose hair):
- No abnormal joins; 0 stretched join edges in every pose and animation frame.
- Flat feet, no heel spikes.
- Valid weights.
- Bending within the frozen limits at elbow, forearm, knee and shoulder.
- The rigged and unrigged files have identical surfaces.

**Frozen verdict:** **NOT USABLE** for both models. The penetration gate G2 fails in 6 of 7 poses for model 2.

**Reviewer finding:** the frozen penetration metric is **unreliable** where body groups share continuous skin (§5). G2 should be read as **NOT VERIFIABLE**, not as a measured failure.

**Collisions between groups that do not share skin** (arm↔leg, hand↔leg) are measurable reliably:
- model 2: **0 in all 8 poses**;
- model 2, wave animation: up to **~3,100 vertices, ≤ 2.2 % H deep** at t ≈ 2.30 s. The animation drives the arms into the thighs.

**Bottom line (INFERRED):** model 2 is a plausible base for *animation previews* once a reliable collision test confirms it. Model 1 is not.

## 3. Method and disclosures

- **Frozen method:** `METHOD_AND_CRITERIA_FROZEN.md` (SHA-256 `8c7cd5840b16232e68a718d4835f20c3566f919a433c43ae8714255661593f73`), frozen before any posed geometry was computed. Applied unchanged to model 2.
- **Implementation clarifications C1–C4,** fixed before any posed result was computed. See the header of `scripts/eval_autorig.py`:
  - **C1:** cross-section distances measured to each vertex's own bone segment.
  - **C2:** shoulder slump measured as lag behind the rigidly moved arm; the frozen window would have been dominated by hair.
  - **C3:** twist distribution 30/60/90° made absolute.
  - **C4:** axis construction for flexion poses.
- **Post-hoc additions,** clearly separate from the frozen verdict:
  - `scripts/joins.py`: cross-region join table, added after the **user pointed out** that the legs are connected to the arms.
  - `scripts/bridge_stretch.py`: join-edge stretch.
  - §5: diagnosis of the penetration metric.
- **Reproducibility:**
  - Model 2 frozen evaluation: run twice, byte-identical (`autorig_eval.json` `95a2bff9…`, contact sheet `0d68f453…`).
  - Bridge stretch: run twice for both models, byte-identical.
  - **Model 1 frozen evaluation was stopped after the 8 poses, during the animation frames.** It is far slower at 981 k vertices, and the model was superseded. Its pose counts (§4.1) are a **single partial run, indicative only**.
- **Tools:** Python 3, numpy, scipy, Pillow. Linear blend skinning computed directly from the glTF skin; no Blender.

## 5. Why the frozen penetration gate is unreliable (reviewer finding, post-hoc)

The frozen metric scores each vertex against its nearest vertex in a *different* body group, signed by that vertex's normal. Three observations from the committed output show it misfires where groups share continuous skin:
1. **Model 2's rest pose scores 6,288 "penetrating" vertices, up to 9.4 % H deep,** on an unposed mesh. These sit at the armpit, groin, wrist, ankle and neck creases.
2. **In P1, P2 and P3, which move only the arms, leg|torso counts are identical (310 L, 578 R) although the legs do not move.** Moving the arms changes which other-group vertex is nearest to a hip vertex, and that flips its status. This is an artifact of the measurement, not a collision.
3. **Every gated pose failure for model 2 is an adjacent pair.** There are no arm|leg or hand|leg contacts in any pose.

**Consequence:** G2 is **NOT VERIFIABLE** for both models. The frozen verdicts are reported as computed and are not re-scored.

**Fix:** a v2 method should test only for intersections between groups that don't share skin, or use proper triangle-intersection tests. It must be frozen before it is run.

## 6. Recommendations (proposals; user decides)

1. **Use model 2, not model 1,** as the base for any auto-rig preview work. Model 1's fused arms and hair rule it out for arm motion.
2. **Before relying on model 2 for previews,** run a **v2 collision test**, frozen in advance: triangle-triangle intersection between groups that don't share skin, plus crease-aware self-contact. Confirm the 0-collision result in poses and quantify the wave animation's arm-into-thigh contacts.
3. **Pick or author animations for these proportions.** `greet_02` drives the arms into the wide hips and thighs.
4. **Keep the Tripo rig out of production.** It has no hair or jaw bones, and its proportions are not checked against the locked v10 rig. That comparison needs the v10 bone JSON (`eab1839c…`), held on BlendBro's machine and not on GitHub.
5. **Store model 2's files privately** with their hashes. Tripo terms apply.
